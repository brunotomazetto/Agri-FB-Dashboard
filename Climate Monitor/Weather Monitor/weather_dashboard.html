Source: NASA POWER / ERA5 (selectable model, historical) + Open-Meteo (observed, to bridge the gap since the last historical refresh). Observed data only — no forecast: the series ends on the last day that has actually happened. Always the current year vs. the distribution of recent years. Thick line = current year · thin = individual years · shading = min–max range · dashed = mean. Temperature = monthly/weekly mean · Rainfall = monthly/weekly total. A multi-point average has a naturally narrow band (spatial cancellation) — select a single point to see the real spread. My farms: your own clusters are stored in your account, not in this browser — signing out or clearing the cache does not lose them, and they stay until you delete them. Only you can see them. Each point snaps to the NASA POWER grid cell (0.5° × 0.625°, ~55 × 67 km) that contains it, so two farms less than ~50 km apart legitimately share the same series. State groups (RS · PR · MS · MT · GO · MATOPIBA): each region row is averaged over every municipality it contains (2,359 in total, 51 regions) — MT by the 7 Imea macro-regions (official municipal list, 141/141), the other eight states by IBGE meso-regions. MATOPIBA pools MA, TO, PI and BA, and opens buttons to isolate each. The three ▣ rows are the state mean under different municipal weights: simple, corn output and soybean output (IBGE/SIDRA 1612, average of 2022–24 to damp a single bad harvest) — the crop-weighted ones pull the state average toward where that crop actually is, which matters most where output is concentrated (Extremo Oeste Baiano alone is 99.6% of BA soybean). The live observed layer samples the highest-weight grid cells of each region; the historical series uses all of them.
